# Fursah — three-minute pitch

## 0:00–0:30 — The decision

Students already generate academic evidence, employers publish changing skill needs, and universities want better outcome signals. The problem is that these streams rarely meet. Fursah connects them into one traceable decision chain: evidence, inference, readiness, and action.

## 0:30–1:05 — The trust boundary

The score is deterministic and versioned. Generative AI does not calculate readiness, rank people, verify evidence, or invent achievements. It explains facts already stored for the signed-in user. No source evidence means no claim, and low confidence stays visible.

## 1:05–1:40 — The standards proof

The prototype maps all seven ITU-T Y.3172 machine-learning pipeline nodes to running components. It also assesses all thirteen readiness dimensions with addressed, partial, or out-of-scope labels. Judges can inspect every mapping on the Standards page and follow the implementation evidence.

## 1:40–2:15 — The contribution

Fursah publishes a reusable knowledge base with seventeen authentic public sources, implementation consequences, and JSON and CSV exports. It converts six observed gaps into operational recommendations, each with an owner, trigger, success metric, and review cadence.

## 2:15–2:50 — The live proof

Open the judge route, enter the prepared student account, reconstruct the readiness score, and ask the grounded assistant to explain it. Then switch to the administrator view to inspect human approval, appeals, overrides, privacy suppression, and the Y.3172 trace.

## 2:50–3:00 — Close

Fursah makes workforce-readiness evidence actionable while keeping every important claim inspectable. The next step is a controlled institutional pilot—not an unsupported production-impact claim.
